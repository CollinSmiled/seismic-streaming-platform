# Live EMSC ingestion

The Python service connects to EMSC's documented `wss://www.seismicportal.eu/standing_order/websocket` endpoint. Each `create` or `update` envelope passes through the same parser and version 1 event contract as the captured catalogue fixture. The Kafka key is the EMSC `unid`; the processor's revision ordering and upsert handle duplicate delivery.

The service keeps one Kafka producer and waits for the delivery callback before reading another WebSocket message. Kafka uses idempotent production and `acks=all`. This one-record-at-a-time choice favors simple, bounded memory over maximum throughput. The producer queue is capped at 1,000 records, the WebSocket receive buffer at four frames, each source message at 64 KiB, and Kafka acknowledgement waiting at 15 seconds. A full local queue is polled until it has room or the deadline expires.

A dropped WebSocket or failed Kafka delivery closes the source connection, then the service reconnects after exponential delays from 1 to 30 seconds. Ctrl+C or SIGTERM stops new reads; an in-progress acknowledgement and shutdown flush have finite timeouts. Invalid source messages are logged without their raw content and skipped.

An observed message may be retried after an uncertain acknowledgement, so duplicates are possible and the processor must remain idempotent. The WebSocket cannot replay notifications missed while Kafka is unavailable or the connection is down. The separate reconciliation worker asks [EMSC's FDSN event service](https://www.seismicportal.eu/fdsn-wsevent.html) for events updated after its saved checkpoint, sends them through the same contract and Kafka topic, then saves the scan start time only after all Kafka acknowledgements. If a scan fails, the checkpoint stays put and the next scan repeats it; the database projection ignores duplicate or stale revisions.

The worker initially looks back one hour. Later requests start 15 minutes before the previous successful scan. This overlap gives delayed catalogue updates another chance to appear and deliberately repeats some events. One scan accepts at most 5,000 features and 16 MiB of response data by default. A saturated or malformed response fails without advancing the checkpoint; the worker retries and an operator can increase `--max-events` up to 19,999 or investigate the source. This bounded single-request design avoids unstable offset pagination over a changing catalogue. It does not provide a perfect snapshot of EMSC's index: updates indexed more than 15 minutes late, or events older than the first lookback, can still be missed. A later observability increment will expose sustained failures as metrics and alerts.

The deterministic tests use a local WebSocket, Kafka, a mocked FDSN response, and an isolated PostgreSQL database. A manual EMSC WebSocket handshake confirms connectivity, but a real earthquake notification cannot be scheduled for CI.
